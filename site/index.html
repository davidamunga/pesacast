M-PESA payments, on screen

Every payment on your screen the second it lands.

A phone receives the M-PESA message. PesaCast reads it and shows it on your computer, or posts it to your own server. That is all it does.

Free and open source. An Android phone, plus a Mac or Windows computer if you want the desktop view.

What it does

Three steps, none of them clever.

  1. The phone reads the message.

    Safaricom texts the phone for every payment. PesaCast, running on that phone, picks up messages from MPESA and turns each into an amount, a name, a reference and a balance.

  2. It sends it where you point it.

    To your computer over Bluetooth or your own Wi-Fi. Or to a web address you control, called a webhook. Or both. Nothing goes through a server of ours, because there is not one.

  3. Your screen shows it.

    A notification, and a live list on the desktop app. Nothing to refresh. Export the list to Excel when you need it.

What it does not do

Said up front, because it matters more than the list above.

It is not proof of payment.

PesaCast forwards a text message. A text can be faked, delayed, or lost if the phone was off. If money or goods depend on a payment, confirm it with Safaricom’s Daraja callbacks and use PesaCast alongside them.

It never touches your money or your PIN.

It cannot send money, cannot send SMS, and never asks for your M-PESA PIN. It only receives what arrives.

It is not from Safaricom.

Independent, unofficial, and not affiliated with or endorsed by Safaricom.

It is not on the Play Store.

You install it from a file. The setup guide shows how, and checks the file against a checksum before it goes on the phone.

It will not make a cheap phone behave.

Many budget phones stop background apps to save battery. The setup checklist walks you through the settings that keep PesaCast alive, but some are menus only you can open. See phones.

It only understands M-PESA.

Other wallets and banks send different messages. They are ignored.

Where your data goes

To the desktop app, straight from the phone, over an encrypted link on your own network. To your webhook, only if you turn it on, over HTTPS, to the address you typed. Nowhere else.

The desktop app checks GitHub for new versions. The code is open, so you can read exactly what the phone does with a message: how it stays yours.

Running a shop or an office?

About fifteen minutes: put the app on a spare Android phone, put the desktop app on a computer, pair them.
